We grow food, then grow materials from what is left

Plizone is a farm and materials studio in Singapore. We grow five varieties of oyster mushroom indoors, sell fresh vegetables and seafood alongside them, and turn spent mushroom blocks into panels for walls and ceilings.

Oyster mushrooms grow on agricultural waste such as sugarcane bagasse. After harvest, each block is laced with mycelium, the root network of the fungus. That network is the glue in our panels, so what the farm leaves behind becomes the next product instead of rubbish.

How we work

Grow

Sterile inoculation, dark incubation and humid fruiting rooms, with temperature and humidity set for each variety.

In development

Sense

Sensors and a camera watch every bag for contamination and growth stage, so each harvest improves the next recipe.

Make

Spent blocks are broken up, packed into moulds, grown for about a week, then oven-dried at 60 to 80 °C.

Who we are

A small team across mycology, engineering, materials science and business. Our acoustic and thermal testing began as a final-year research project at NTU, and we keep testing as we grow.
